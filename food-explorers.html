How this worked for one business

After-school cooking classes, run out of an inbox

Food Explorers runs after-school cooking and baking classes in schools across the Bay Area. The classes were never the hard part. Everything around them was.

Business

After-school cooking and baking classes

Where

Schools across the Bay Area

Run by

The founder, with instructors on contract

Started with

Google Drive, email and text messages

What a normal week looked like

Every one of these was done by hand, by one person, every season.

Hiring an instructor, then walking them through onboarding from scratch.

Training them, and getting them up to speed on how each school wants things done.

Sending recipes and instructions out before every class.

Working out schedules with schools and with parents.

Writing and sending invoices to each school, one at a time.

Remembering which background checks each school required, and who had finished them.

The documents themselves lived in Google Drive, which worked. The asking, the chasing and the remembering lived somewhere less reliable: a mix of email, text messages and the founder’s memory.

None of it was broken, exactly. It just all ran through one person, and it all ran again from the start every season.

The part that couldn’t slip

Compliance. Before an instructor is allowed on a school site, that school’s requirements have to be met: background checks, fingerprinting, whatever else that district asks for. Different schools want different things, in different forms, on different timelines.

A late invoice is an annoyance. An instructor turned away at the door on the first day of class is a different kind of problem, and it lands on the school’s opinion of you as much as on the schedule.

Tracked by hand, per instructor, per school, in an inbox. That is where most of the risk sat.

This is the thing worth noticing: the founder wasn’t behind because she was disorganised. She was behind because the work had no home outside her own attention.

Where we started

Not with software. With a walk through a normal week, to find which parts were actually costing her.

Three things came out of that. Onboarding took too many rounds of back-and-forth. Each school’s compliance rules existed only in her head. And there was no single place to see where the season stood.

So we built those three, and left everything else alone.

What we built

01

Onboarding that asks once

A new instructor now gets one clear list of everything to send, up front, instead of discovering it over four rounds of email. What’s arrived and what’s still missing is visible without anyone having to ask.

02

A compliance checklist for each school

Every school’s requirements written down once, in one place, and tracked per instructor. When a school asks whether someone is cleared to be on site, the answer is on a screen rather than in a memory.

03

One view of the season

Every school in the program, the classes running at each, which instructor is locked in, and which classes still have nobody. The things that need a decision sit at the top.

What changed

The founder opens one page and knows where the season stands: which classes still need an instructor, whose paperwork is outstanding, and which school is closest to being a problem.

The work that used to live in her attention now lives somewhere she can look at it.

One real number goes here before this page is published: hours a week returned, days saved per instructor onboarded, or rounds of email removed. Ask her for the figure; don’t estimate it.

What we didn’t change

  • Google Drive stayed. The documents still live where they always did.
  • No new tool for instructors to learn before they can teach.
  • The accounts are in her name. We can be removed without anything breaking.

Sound like your business?

Food Explorers teaches cooking. The same shape shows up in martial-arts schools, music schools, tutoring centres, home-service companies and anywhere else that sends trained people out to other people’s sites.
